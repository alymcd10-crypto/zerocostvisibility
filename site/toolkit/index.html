Step 01 · They post about me

Hand them the post.

Don't ask sellers to share the listing. Give them the words. On listing day, pick the one that fits your seller's voice and text it to them. They post. You wrote it. Buyers can post up to three times: keys day, settling in, and the renovation reveal. Offer, never require. Swap in your name and number before you send.

01 School and neighborhood

Seller
Our beautiful home is on the market. [Neighborhood and school highlight, e.g. Oak Grove schools and an amazing neighborhood]. To set up a private showing, call [Your name] (our realtor) at [your phone].

Best for family communities and school-district markets.

02 Top local expert

Seller
Now available in the heart of [town or neighborhood]. For a private showing, contact top local expert [Your name] at [your phone].

Best for walkable downtowns and lifestyle listings.

03 Shortest

Seller
Please call my agent, [Your name], for a private showing at [your phone].

Best for clients who don't want to write much.

04 The personal story

Seller
We bought this home [X] years ago and it has been everything we hoped for. Time for the next chapter. If you or someone you love is looking in [town], message me, or call our realtor [Your name] at [your phone].

Best for longtime owners with an emotional story. Carries the most feeling.

05 Before it goes live

Seller
Coming to the market in [town] next week: [X bed, Y bath, key feature]. If you know anyone looking, DM me or reach out directly to [Your name] at [your phone]. Happy to do a private walkthrough first.

Best for building interest early. Check your brokerage and MLS rules before any pre-market post.

06 Keys day

Buyer
We did it. After [length of search] we finally found our home in [town]. So grateful to [Your name] with [your team and brokerage] for finding it and getting our offer accepted in a multiple-offer situation. If you're looking in the area, [Your name] is the one to call: [your phone].

Best for buyers who beat out multiple offers.

07 New to town

Buyer
Officially residents of [town]. We're so excited to be here. Huge thank-you to our realtor [Your name] for making the move feel manageable from across the country. If anyone has restaurant, preschool or contractor recommendations, send them our way. And if you're house-hunting here, our realtor is the one: [your phone].

Best for relocation buyers. Doubles as a we-belong-here post.

08 The renovation reveal

Buyer
Six months in our [town] home and the [kitchen / yard / basement] is finally done. Sharing because so many of you helped, including [Your name], who connected us with [contractor] the week we moved in. Still recommending [Your name] to anyone who'll listen: [your phone].

Best 6 to 12 months after closing. A second wave from the same client.

Step 02 · They review me

The message I actually send.

Send it the week of closing, while they're still excited about the keys. Send it by email, or by text if you've been texting throughout the deal. If you haven't heard back, remind them on day 5 and day 10, then stop.

Review request by text or email

Hi friends. Can you do me a big favor and do this? ❤️ Would you mind leaving us a review on both Google and Zillow? Reviews truly help my business and help future buyers and sellers find us! Please use your own words. Personal details about your experience are always the most meaningful. 😊 ⭐ Zillow: [Your Zillow review link] ⭐ Google: [Your Google review link] Thank you so much for taking the time to do this. We truly appreciate your business, your trust, and your support! ❤️

Swap in your own Zillow and Google review links before you send.

The follow-up is the engine

Most reviews aren't written because the client forgot, not because they don't want to. Two gentle reminders over ten days, then a stop date.

Day 5 The gentle reminder

If no review yet
Hey [name], checking in. Wanted to make sure my last note didn't get buried. If you have a minute, an honest review on Google or Zillow would mean a lot. Your own words are the most helpful. Links: [Google + Zillow review links]

Send on day 5 if there is no review yet.

Day 10 One more

Last message
Hi [name], one more from me, then I'll leave it with you. If you have a minute, here are the links. Totally fine either way. Links: [Google + Zillow review links]

Send on day 10 if there is still no review. This is the last message.

Day 14 The stop date

Nothing sent

No message. If there is no review by day 14, let it go. Mark it in the tracker and move on.

A decline, or silence, is an answer. Respect it.

Ask once, remind twice, stop at day 14.

If a client freezes at the blank page

Only if they ask for help. They write it, a tool tidies spelling and clarity, and they read it before posting. The experience, the facts and the opinion stay theirs.

For the client to use on their own draft

Optional
Here is a review I wrote about my real estate agent. [paste your draft] Please fix spelling, punctuation and clarity only. Do not add facts, claims, names or opinions. Keep my tone and roughly my length. Return one version.

Send this to the client only if they ask. Never write the review for them.

One review, thirty days of visibility

When a five-star review lands, brand it once and run this schedule.

  1. Day 0

    Capture the review

    Screenshot it on Zillow or Google. Crop to the five stars and first paragraph. Add your logo and the date in Canva.

  2. Day 1

    Instagram feed

    Two-image carousel: the branded review and a photo from closing day. The caption names the client, the moment and the town.

  3. Day 2

    Instagram Story

    Same image. Tag the client if they're comfortable. Add a "message me to be next" sticker.

  4. Day 3

    Facebook, personal page

    Same image, longer caption. Talk about what the work felt like.

  5. Day 5

    Facebook, business page

    Re-share with a slightly different first sentence.

  6. Day 7

    One community group

    Only where it's allowed, or where you're a neighbor and not pitching.

  7. Day 10

    LinkedIn

    A LinkedIn-native post with no link in the body. Frame it as what referrals make possible.

  8. Day 14

    Email signature

    Swap your signature image for the new review for the next 30 days.

  9. Day 17

    Pinterest quote

    Pull the strongest sentence into a quote graphic for a "Why clients choose us" board.

  10. Day 21

    Stories highlight

    Move the original story into your "Reviews" highlight for good.

  11. Day 28

    Newsletter

    If you send a monthly email, the review goes in the next one.

Step 03 · They recommend me

The ninety-second ask.

When someone posts "anyone know a realtor?" in a local Facebook group, text three to five past clients who live there. Turn on post alerts for 8 to 10 community groups and keep your past clients sorted by neighborhood, so you can send the ask within 90 seconds.

Score every group first

Before you spend a year being active in a group, ask four questions: how many members, how active it is, what the rules are, and whether you can show up as a neighbor. Then score it below, 0 to 2 points per row, out of 10. Print this page; one column per group.

Then send the ask

For your top 10 to 15 raving fans. They don't need context.

02 Recent clients

Text
(Send a screenshot of the post) Would you mind chiming in on this group if you can?

For clients you closed with in the last 12 months.

Text

For clients from 2+ years ago. Warmth plus a reminder of who you are.

04 When they ask how to help

In person
Honestly? The most valuable thing you could do is post about our experience on your Facebook page. Tag me if you're comfortable, or just mention me by name. Those community posts are how people find me.

For when the client offers first. Always say: make the post.

Be findable

Be everywhere they look.

When someone asks a search engine or a chatbot for a realtor, it works from what is public about you. Four ways to make accurate information about your work easier to find.

  1. 01 · Reviews

    Ask every client the week of closing, with both links one tap away. Remind twice, then stop. Keep Google and Zillow both current, and reply to every review in your own words.

  2. 02 · Recommendations

    When a client recommends you in a public group or page, that mention can be found. In a private group it works on people, not tools. Say thank you under every one.

  3. 03 · Local content

    Post what you'd post anyway: trick-or-treat lists, market updates, neighborhood guides, a short blog post. Your name and your town together in one more place. One a month is plenty.

  4. 04 · Accurate profiles

    Same name, towns, phone and email on Zillow, Google, your brokerage page, LinkedIn and social. Once a quarter, ask ChatGPT and Claude "best realtor in [your town]" and note what they say. No tool promises who it will name.

Write listings buyers can actually use

Vague listings are hard to find and hard to read. Use this prompt to turn any listing into clear, specific copy built only from facts you have verified.

Specifics beat poetry. The tool drafts; you check every fact and every fair housing line before it goes in the MLS.

Listing rewrite prompt

Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
Rewrite this MLS listing description so a buyer can understand it quickly. Keep it plain, specific and natural. Original listing: [paste current MLS description] Facts to use exactly as written (I have verified them): · Town and neighborhood or subdivision: [ ] · Bedrooms / bathrooms: [ ] · Square footage: [ ] · Year built: [ ] · Lot size: [ ] · School district and assigned schools, names only: [ ] · Miles to the train station / downtown: [ ] · Major upgrades, with the year completed: [ ] · 3 features buyers here ask about (yard, finished basement, garage): [ ] Rules: · Use only the facts above and the original listing. Do not add, guess or round anything. If something is missing, ask me. · Describe the property, never the people who might live there. No "perfect for families," and no opinions about schools, safety or the neighborhood. · Open with one plain sentence that says what the home is. · Write in sentences, not bullets. Skip empty adjectives ("stunning," "must-see," "gorgeous"). · No agent or brokerage names, phone numbers, websites, open house or showing details. Give me two versions: one under 1,000 characters for the MLS remarks field (I will adjust to my MLS limit), and one of about 250 words for my website and social posts. Then list anything you were unsure of so I can check it.

For every past client

The first year after closing.

Six touches, timed from each client's closing date. None of them ask for anything.

  1. Week of closing

    A closing gift

    Hand-delivered, not mailed. Pick something they'll want to photograph and post.

  2. Day 30

    A check-in text

    "One month in. How's the house?" Listen, solve one thing, and connect them with a contractor if they need one.

  3. Month 3

    Something useful

    "Just heard about [tax appeal deadline / school open house / new restaurant] and thought of you."

  4. Month 6

    A coffee invitation

    "It's been six months. Free for coffee this week?" Most people say no, and that's fine. The invitation is the point.

  5. Month 9

    A seasonal home tip

    "Reminder to drain your outdoor spigots this week," or the name of the gutter person you use.

  6. Month 12

    An anniversary card

    Handwritten and mailed. "A year ago today you got the keys. I'm here whenever you need me."
