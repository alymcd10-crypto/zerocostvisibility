Step 01 · They post about me

Hand them the post.

Don't ask sellers to share the listing. Give them the words. Copy, paste, hand off. On the day of listing, pick the one that fits the seller's voice and text it to them. They post. You wrote it. Swap in your name and number before you send.

Seller posts (five)

01 School and neighborhood

Seller
Our beautiful home is on the market. [Neighborhood and school highlight, e.g. Oak Grove schools and an amazing neighborhood]. To set up a private showing, call our realtor [Your name] at [your phone].

Best for: family communities, school-district markets.

02 Top local expert

Seller
Now available in the heart of [town or neighborhood]. For a private showing, contact top local expert [Your name] at [your phone].

Best for: walkable downtowns, lifestyle-driven listings.

03 Shortest: my agent

Seller
Please call my agent, [Your name], for a private showing at [your phone].

Best for: clients who don't want to write much. Frictionless.

04 The personal story

Seller
We bought this home [X] years ago and it has been everything we hoped for. Time for the next chapter. If you or someone you love is looking in [town], message me, or call our realtor [Your name] at [your phone].

Best for: longtime owners with an emotional story. Carries the most feeling.

05 The 'before it goes live' tease

Seller
Coming to the market in [town] next week: [X bed, Y bath, key feature]. If you know anyone looking, DM me or reach out directly to [Your name] at [your phone]. Happy to do a private walkthrough first.

Best for: building interest early. Check your brokerage and MLS rules before any pre-market post.

Buyer posts (three)

Sellers get the listing post. Buyers get the moving-in post. Offer them; never require them. Sellers post once. Buyers can post up to three times: closing, settling in, the renovation reveal.

06 The keys-day post

Buyer
We did it. After [length of search] we finally found our home in [town]. Eternally grateful to [Your name with your team and brokerage] for finding it for us and getting our offer accepted in a multi-offer situation. If you're looking in the area, [Your name] is the one to call: [your phone].

Suggested window: keys week. Best for clients who beat out multiple offers.

07 The 'new to town' post

Buyer
Officially residents of [town]. We're so excited to be here. Huge thank-you to our realtor [Your name] for making the relocation feel manageable from across the country. If anyone has restaurant, preschool, or contractor recommendations, please send them our way. And if you're house-hunting in the area, our realtor is the one: [your phone].

Best for: relocation buyers. Doubles as a 'we belong here' announcement.

08 The renovation reveal (months later)

Buyer
Six months in our [town] home and finally the [kitchen / yard / basement] is done. Sharing because so many of you helped, including [Your name], who connected us with [contractor name] the week we moved in. Still recommending [Your name] to anyone who'll listen: [your phone].

Best for: 6 to 12 months after closing. A second wave of visibility from the same client.

Step 02 · They recommend me

The recommendation ask.

When someone posts "anyone know a realtor?" in a community group, text a few past clients in the area and the comments fill up. Four variants, calibrated to your relationship with the client.

The suggested setup. Turn on post alerts for the community groups your clients use. Organize past clients by neighborhood, three to five names per group, so you know whom to contact when a relevant request appears.

A Variant A: closest clients

Text
(Send a screenshot of the post) Please chime in if you can.

For: your top 10 to 15 raving fans. They get the short version because they don't need context.

B Variant B: recent clients

Text
(Send a screenshot of the post) Would you mind chiming in on this group if you can?

For: clients you closed with in the last 12 months. Slightly more deferential.

C Variant C: long-ago clients

Text
(Send a screenshot) Hi [name], saw this post in [group] and thought of you. Would you mind dropping a quick comment if you're up for it? Totally no pressure either way.

For: clients from 2+ years ago. Needs warmth plus a reminder of who you are.

D Variant D: 'How can I help?'

In person
Honestly? The most valuable thing you could do is post about our experience on your Facebook page. Tag me if you're comfortable, or just mention me by name. Those community posts are how people find me.

For: when a thrilled client asks you directly what they can do, and makes the offer first. Always say: make the post.

Step 03 · They review me

The message I actually send.

Send it the week of closing, while the keys-in-hand feeling is still alive. Send it by email, or by text if you have been texting throughout the deal.

Both links are in the message, one tap each. If they have to go looking, it doesn't happen.

If you haven't heard back, see the follow-up messages. Once a review is in, put it to work with the 30-day reuse schedule.

Review request by text or email

Hi friends. Can you do me a big favor and do this? ❤️ Would you mind leaving us a review on both Google and Zillow? Reviews truly help my business and help future buyers and sellers find us! Please use your own words. Personal details about your experience are always the most meaningful. 😊 ⭐ Zillow: https://www.zillow.com/profile/lizturnbaugh1 ⭐ Google: https://g.page/r/CU163zbxFhsnEAE/review Thank you so much for taking the time to do this. We truly appreciate your business, your trust, and your support! ❤️

Replace both links with your own Zillow and Google review links before you send.

Step 03 · Follow-up messages

The follow-up is the engine.

Most reviews aren't written because the client forgot, not because they don't want to. Two gentle reminders over ten days, then a stop date. Count days from the first ask.

DAY 5 The gentle reminder

Text or email
Hey [name], checking in. Wanted to make sure my last note didn't get buried. If you have a minute, an honest review on Google or Zillow would mean a lot. Your own words are the most helpful. Links: [Google + Zillow review links]

Send Day 5 if no review yet.

DAY 10 One more

Text or email
Hi [name], one more from me, then I'll leave it with you. If you have a minute, here are the links. Totally fine either way. Links: [Google + Zillow review links]

Send Day 10 if still no review. This is the last message.

DAY 14 The stop date

No message

No message. If there is no review by Day 14, let it go. Mark it in the tracker and move on.

A decline, or silence, is an answer. Respect it.

Day 14 is internal. Nothing is sent.

The pattern. Ask once, remind twice, stop at Day 14. The follow-up is what gets it written.

Step 03 · Use every review everywhere

One review. Thirty days of visibility.

When a five-star review lands, brand it once and run this exact schedule. Eleven steps, day by day.

  1. Day 0

    Capture the review

    Screenshot the Zillow or Google review. Crop to the five stars and first paragraph. Brand with your logo and the date in Canva.

  2. Day 1

    Instagram main feed

    Single-image carousel: the branded review plus a photo from closing day. The caption names the client, the moment and the town.

  3. Day 2

    Instagram Story

    Same image. Tag the client if they're comfortable. Add a "message me to be next" sticker.

  4. Day 3

    Facebook personal page

    Same image, longer caption. Talk about what the work felt like.

  5. Day 5

    Facebook business page

    Re-share to your business page with a slightly different lead-in sentence.

  6. Day 7

    One community group

    Share to a group where it's permitted, or one where you're a neighbor, not pitching. Tag yourself only in the caption.

  7. Day 10

    LinkedIn

    Reformat to a LinkedIn-native post, with no link in the body. Frame it as "what referrals make possible."

  8. Day 14

    Email signature swap

    Replace your signature image with the new review for the next 30 days.

  9. Day 17

    Pinterest quote graphic

    Pull the strongest sentence into a quote graphic. Pin it to your "Why Clients Choose Us" board.

  10. Day 21

    Stories highlight

    Move the original Instagram story into your "Reviews" highlight permanently.

  11. Day 28

    Newsletter inclusion

    If you have a monthly email, the review goes in the next send, even if it's months old by then.

The multiplier. One review. An 11-step reuse plan. Thirty days of reach from a single happy client.

Listing prompt

Write listings buyers can actually use.

Vague listings are hard to find and hard to read. Use this prompt to rewrite any listing into clear, specific copy that answers the questions buyers actually ask. Fill in the brackets, then paste it into the AI tool you use.

Rewrite any MLS listing for clarity Prompt

Rewrite the following MLS listing so it is clear, specific and easy for a buyer to understand, and reads naturally. Original listing: [paste current MLS description] Property facts to embed verbatim: · Address (or town if pre-MLS): [ ] · Square footage: [ ] · Year built: [ ] · Lot size: [ ] · Bedrooms / bathrooms: [ ] · School district + specific schools by name: [ ] · Distance to nearest train station / downtown / major employer: [ ] · Major upgrades, year completed: [ ] · 3 things buyers in this area actually ask about (yard, finished basement, garage): [ ] Rewrite to: · Open with a clear, conversational sentence that answers "what is this home?" · Embed every fact above in plain language (not bullet points) · Use the natural-language phrases buyers use ("walkable downtown," "good schools," "finished basement") · Avoid empty adjectives ("stunning," "must-see," "gorgeous") · End with a question buyers might ask AI ("Looking for a [X]-bedroom near [landmark]? This one closes in on it.") Give me two versions: one 150 words, one 300 words.

Why this works. Specifics beat poetry. Square feet, school names, distances and real questions are what people search for.

Optional: help a client polish their own words

Some clients want to leave a review and freeze at the blank page. If they ask for help, this is the only kind to offer: they write it, a tool tidies spelling and clarity, and they read it before posting. The client uses this prompt on their own draft.

Spelling and clarity only Optional · for the client

Here is a review I wrote about my real estate agent. [paste your draft] Please fix spelling, punctuation and clarity only. Do not add facts, claims, names or opinions. Keep my tone and roughly my length. Return one version.

Why this works. The client writes it. The tool only tidies it. The experience, the facts and the opinion stay theirs.

AI search checklist

Be everywhere they look.

When someone asks a search engine or a chatbot for a realtor, it works from what is public about you. Four ways to make accurate information about your work easier to find, with what to do about each. Nobody can promise what a search tool will say.

  1. 01 · Reviews

    Ask every client the week of closing (Step 03). Both links, one tap each. Follow up twice, then stop.

    Keep Google and Zillow both current. Reply to every review in your own words.

    One source of client experience, in their words.

  2. 02 · Recommendations

    Run Step 02. When a client recommends you in a public group or page, that mention can be found. In a private group it works on people, not tools.

    Say thank you under every one.

    The history people scroll is the history tools can read when it is public.

  3. 03 · Local content

    Post the things you would post anyway: trick-or-treat lists, market updates, neighborhood guides, a short blog post.

    Your name and your town, together, in one more place.

    Useful beats clever. One a month is plenty.

  4. 04 · Accurate profiles

    Same name, same towns, same phone and email on Zillow, Google, your brokerage page, LinkedIn and social.

    Check them once a quarter. Ask ChatGPT and Claude "best realtor in [your town]" and note what they say.

    Housekeeping, not the engine. No promises about who any tool will name.

The imprint. Your imprint on the internet gives people, and the tools they ask, accurate information to go on. Reviews and recommendations are the fuel.

For every past client

One year. Six touches. Zero spam.

Six deliberate touchpoints over twelve months. Genuinely useful, never salesy. Run every past client through this calendar and adjust the month for their close date.

  1. Week of closing

    The closing gift

    A personalized gift tied to the home. An anchor moment that sets the tone for every touch after.

  2. Day 30

    The 'how's it feeling' text

    "Hey [name], one month in. How's the house?" Listen. Solve one thing. Connect them to a contractor if needed. Builds the "she's the resource" identity. About 5 minutes.

  3. Month 3

    The unprompted resource share

    "Just learned about [tax appeal deadline / school open house / new restaurant on Milwaukee Ave]. Thought of you." Zero ask. Pure value. Trains them to think of you.

  4. Month 6

    The coffee invitation

    "Hey, it's been half a year. Free for a coffee or a quick walk this week?" Some yes, most no. The ask is the touch.

  5. Month 9

    The seasonal home tip

    "It's getting cold, quick reminder to drain your outdoor spigots," or "Spring is coming, here's the gutter guy I use." Establishes you as the home-knowledge person.

  6. Month 12

    The anniversary card

    Handwritten. Mailed, not texted. "A year ago today you got the keys. Hope you're settled. Here whenever you need me." Most agents stop at 6 months. Be the one who shows up at 12.
